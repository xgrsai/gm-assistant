# Call of Cthulhu 7e Rules Reference

This reference summarises the core mechanics of Call of Cthulhu 7th edition in original wording for the GM Assistant knowledge base. It is not a copy of any Chaosium book. Where the Quick-Start Rules are silent and a rule comes from the full Keeper Rulebook as reported by secondary sources, the text says so. The Keeper always has the final word; a ruling that is not covered here should be announced as a "Keeper ruling".

## Characteristics and Derived Attributes

### The Eight Characteristics

Every investigator has eight characteristics, each normally between 15 and 90:

- STR (Strength): raw muscle power, lifting, forcing doors.
- CON (Constitution): health, stamina, resistance to poison, disease and pain.
- SIZ (Size): height and weight combined.
- DEX (Dexterity): agility and speed; determines who acts first in combat.
- APP (Appearance): attractiveness and charisma.
- INT (Intelligence): cunning, memory and the ability to make connections; used for the Idea roll.
- POW (Power): willpower and spirit; starting Sanity equals POW.
- EDU (Education): formal knowledge; used for the Know roll and the base of the investigator's own language.

A quick way to create characteristics is to assign the values 40, 50, 50, 50, 60, 60, 70 and 80 to the eight characteristics in any order. The rolled method uses 3D6×5 for STR, CON, DEX, APP and POW, and (2D6+6)×5 for SIZ, INT and EDU.

### Half and Fifth Values

Each characteristic and skill has two smaller numbers written next to it: half the value (rounded down) and one fifth of the value (rounded down). A skill of 60 is written as 60 (30/12). These numbers are the targets for Hard and Extreme success.

### Hit Points, Magic Points, Sanity and Luck

- Hit Points (HP) equal (CON + SIZ) divided by 10, rounded down. An investigator with CON 60 and SIZ 50 has 11 HP.
- Magic Points (MP) equal POW divided by 5, rounded down. Spent Magic Points return at a rate of one per hour.
- Starting Sanity (SAN) equals POW. Maximum Sanity is 99 minus the investigator's Cthulhu Mythos skill.
- Luck is rolled as 3D6×5.

### Damage Bonus and Build

Damage Bonus and Build depend on STR + SIZ:

- 2 to 64: Damage Bonus -2, Build -2.
- 65 to 84: Damage Bonus -1, Build -1.
- 85 to 124: no Damage Bonus, Build 0.
- 125 to 164: Damage Bonus +1D4, Build 1.
- 165 to 204: Damage Bonus +1D6, Build 2.

Larger creatures continue the progression (205 to 284 gives +2D6 and Build 3; each further 80 points adds another 1D6 and 1 Build). Build is used for fighting manoeuvres such as grappling, disarming or knocking a foe down.

### Base Values of Some Skills

Dodge starts at half DEX. Language (Own) starts equal to EDU. Cthulhu Mythos starts at 0 and should only rise through contact with the Mythos. Credit Rating reflects wealth and social standing rather than a practical ability.

## Skill Rolls

### When to Roll

Dice are rolled only when the outcome of an action is uncertain and failure would be dramatic, costly or interesting. Routine actions that a competent person would accomplish, or actions with no meaningful consequence for failure, simply succeed. The Keeper decides which skill applies before the dice are rolled and tells the player what is at stake.

### Rolling Percentile Dice

A skill roll uses two ten-sided dice: one for the tens digit and one for the units digit, giving a result from 1 to 100 (a result of 00 on the tens die and 0 on the units die reads as 100). The roll succeeds if the result is equal to or lower than the number required.

### Difficulty Levels

The Keeper sets the difficulty before the roll:

- Regular difficulty: the roll must be equal to or below the full skill value. This is the default for most tasks.
- Hard difficulty: the roll must be equal to or below half the skill value. Use it when circumstances are adverse or when resisting a competent opponent.
- Extreme difficulty: the roll must be equal to or below one fifth of the skill value. Use it for feats at the edge of human ability.

When an investigator acts against a non-player character outside combat, the Keeper can set the difficulty from the opponent's skill instead of rolling for both sides: Regular if the opposing skill is below 50, Hard if it is 50 or higher, and Extreme if it is 90 or higher (Keeper Rulebook guidance reported by secondary sources).

### Levels of Success

Whatever difficulty was set, the result also shows how well the investigator did:

- Critical success: a roll of 01. The best possible outcome, with an extra benefit if the situation allows.
- Extreme success: a roll at or below one fifth of the skill.
- Hard success: a roll at or below half of the skill.
- Regular success: a roll at or below the skill.
- Failure: a roll above the skill.
- Fumble: the worst possible outcome.

A roll of 100 is always a fumble. When the number needed for success is below 50, any roll from 96 to 100 is a fumble. (The Keeper Rulebook wording refers to the number required for success; this project applies it to the difficulty-adjusted target, so a Hard roll against a skill of 60, which needs 30 or less, fumbles on 96 to 100.) A fumble cannot be pushed and Luck cannot be spent to avoid it.

### Bonus and Penalty Dice

Circumstances can help or hinder an action without changing its difficulty. For each bonus die, roll an additional tens die and keep the lowest tens result. For each penalty die, roll an additional tens die and keep the highest tens result. The units die is rolled only once and is shared by all tens dice.

One bonus die and one penalty die cancel each other out, so a character with two bonus dice and one penalty die rolls with a single bonus die. After cancelling, a roll never uses more than two bonus dice or two penalty dice.

Typical reasons for a bonus die are ample time, excellent tools or a well-prepared plan. Typical reasons for a penalty die are darkness, injury, haste, distraction or unfamiliar equipment.

### Pushing a Roll

After a failed skill or characteristic roll, the player may ask to push it: to try again with more effort, more time or a riskier method. The player must describe what the investigator does differently, and the Keeper should say what could go wrong before the dice are rolled.

- A roll may be pushed only once, and only straight after the failure it tries to overcome.
- If the pushed roll succeeds, the investigator achieves the goal.
- If the pushed roll fails, the Keeper imposes a serious consequence: injury, loss of equipment, drawing unwanted attention, wasting precious time, or worse.
- Combat rolls, Luck rolls, Sanity rolls, Sanity loss rolls and damage rolls cannot be pushed. Opposed rolls and chase actions cannot be pushed either (Keeper Rulebook, via secondary sources).
- A fumble cannot be pushed.

### Opposed Rolls

When two characters directly compete (for example arm wrestling or a battle of wills), both roll against their relevant skill or characteristic and compare levels of success. Extreme beats Hard, Hard beats Regular, and any success beats a failure. If both achieve the same level, the one with the higher skill or characteristic wins. If those values are also equal, both roll 1D100 and the lower roll wins, or the Keeper declares a stalemate.

## Common Investigator Skills

### Perception Skills

- Spot Hidden: noticing hidden or easily missed details by sight, such as a secret compartment in a desk, a concealed door, a clue on a body, a figure in the shadows, or someone following the investigator. Searching a room thoroughly usually calls for Spot Hidden; a quick glance at something in plain view needs no roll.
- Listen: hearing faint or distant sounds, understanding a muffled conversation behind a door, noticing someone approaching, or being woken by a noise at night.
- Psychology: reading a person's intentions and emotions, spotting a lie or concealed fear. The Keeper often rolls Psychology secretly, because a failure should not tell the player that the answer is wrong.

### Knowledge and Research Skills

- Library Use: finding a particular book, newspaper report, record or piece of information in a library, archive, collection of papers or a disordered study. Reading through a stack of notes or a diary for relevant entries is Library Use; understanding a specialist text may require a relevant knowledge skill as well.
- History: knowing the history of a place, family, building or event, and recognising the age of an object.
- Occult: knowledge of folklore, secret societies, witchcraft, spiritualism and occult books. Occult does not cover real Mythos knowledge.
- Cthulhu Mythos: understanding of the true, inhuman Mythos. It is never rolled to research ordinary things, and gaining it costs Sanity.
- Medicine and Science: diagnosing illness and injuries, determining the cause of death, identifying chemicals or preserved specimens.
- Language (Other): reading, writing and speaking a foreign language; old or technical texts may be Hard.

### Social Skills

- Persuade: convincing someone through reasoned argument over a longer conversation; the effect can last.
- Fast Talk: quick verbal trickery that makes someone go along with the investigator for a short while; the target may realise later.
- Charm: winning someone over through friendliness, flattery or attraction.
- Intimidate: frightening or pressuring someone with threats or a show of force.
- Credit Rating: wealth and social standing; it can open doors, buy help, or impress officials and servants.

### Physical and Practical Skills

- Stealth: moving quietly and unseen, hiding, or sneaking past a guard.
- Locksmith: opening locks without the key, repairing locks, or finding a way through a locked door or window.
- Climb and Jump: climbing walls, ropes or trees, and jumping gaps or down from heights. Ordinary stairs and ladders need no roll.
- First Aid: immediate treatment of injuries (see Hit Points, Wounds and Healing).
- Drive Auto, Mechanical Repair, Electrical Repair: operating and fixing machines and vehicles of the period.
- Dodge: avoiding an attack or a falling object; also used for fleeing through a crowd.

## Characteristic Rolls, Idea Rolls and Know Rolls

A characteristic can be rolled like a skill: STR to force a stuck door, CON to resist poison or stay conscious, DEX to catch a falling object, POW to resist mental domination.

- The Idea roll is an INT roll. When the investigators are stuck and have missed an important clue, the players can ask for an Idea roll. On a success the Keeper points them in the right direction; on a failure they still get the clue, but it comes with a cost or a complication.
- The Know roll is an EDU roll used to see whether the investigator happens to know a piece of general knowledge.

## Luck

### Luck Rolls

A Luck roll is made when the outcome depends on pure chance rather than skill, for example whether a taxi happens to pass or whether the right book is on the shelf. Roll 1D100: equal to or below the current Luck value succeeds. When chance affects the whole group, the investigator with the lowest Luck makes the roll.

### Spending Luck (Optional Rule)

With this optional rule from the Keeper Rulebook (reported by secondary sources), a player may spend Luck points after a skill or characteristic roll to lower the result point for point until it succeeds. Luck cannot be spent on Luck rolls, Sanity rolls, Sanity loss rolls or damage rolls, cannot be spent on a pushed roll, and cannot turn a fumble into a success. A success bought with Luck does not earn an experience check for the skill. At the end of a session each investigator rolls 1D100; if the result is higher than current Luck, Luck recovers by 1D10 (to a maximum of 99).

## Sanity

### Sanity Rolls

When an investigator witnesses something horrifying or unnatural, the Keeper calls for a Sanity roll: roll 1D100 against current Sanity points. A roll at or below current SAN succeeds.

### Sanity Loss Notation

Sanity loss is written as two values separated by a slash, such as 0/1D6 or 1/1D8. The number before the slash is lost on a successful Sanity roll; the number after the slash is lost on a failed one. Example values:

- 0/1: an unexpected corpse, a disturbing noise in an empty house.
- 0/1D3: finding gnawed human bones.
- 0/1D6: a gruesome murder scene, witnessing an unnatural creature at a distance.
- 1/1D8 or more: seeing a Mythos creature clearly, watching a friend being devoured.

A fumbled Sanity roll loses the maximum possible amount (Keeper Rulebook, via secondary sources). Sanity loss can never drop current Sanity below zero.

### Involuntary Action

When a Sanity roll fails, the investigator loses self-control for a moment. The Keeper decides the investigator's immediate reaction: screaming, freezing, dropping a lamp, stumbling back, firing wildly. After this involuntary action the player regains control unless insanity sets in.

### Temporary Insanity

If an investigator loses 5 or more Sanity points from a single Sanity roll, the player makes an INT roll. Counter-intuitively, a success is bad news: the investigator fully understands the significance of what was seen and becomes temporarily insane for 1D10 hours of game time. On a failure the mind refuses to process the horror, and the investigator stays sane but may repress the memory.

### Indefinite and Permanent Insanity

- Indefinite insanity: if an investigator loses one fifth or more of the Sanity points they had at the start of the current game day within that day, they become indefinitely insane (Keeper Rulebook, via secondary sources). This lasts for months of game time and usually removes the investigator from play until treated.
- Permanent insanity: an investigator reduced to 0 Sanity becomes permanently insane and passes under the Keeper's control.

### Bouts of Madness

When insanity strikes, the investigator suffers a bout of madness. Played out in real time it lasts 1D10 rounds; summarised, it lasts 1D10 hours. Typical bouts are amnesia, a psychosomatic blindness or paralysis, sudden violence, paranoia, clinging to a significant person, fainting, fleeing in panic, uncontrollable hysterics, a new phobia or a new mania. While insane, any further Sanity loss can trigger another bout.

### Cthulhu Mythos Knowledge and Maximum Sanity

Learning the truth about the Mythos raises the Cthulhu Mythos skill. Every point of Cthulhu Mythos lowers maximum Sanity by one, so an investigator with Cthulhu Mythos 12 can never have more than 87 Sanity.

### Recovering Sanity

Sanity can be regained by defeating Mythos threats (the scenario sets the reward), through psychiatric treatment over long periods, and when a skill improves to 90% or more during the development phase (+2D6 SAN, Keeper Rulebook via secondary sources).

## Combat

### Combat Rounds and Order

Combat is divided into rounds of a few seconds. Characters act in order of DEX, highest first. A character with a firearm already drawn and ready acts as if their DEX were 50 points higher.

### Melee Attacks

The attacker rolls Fighting (Brawl) or another Fighting skill. The defender chooses to fight back or to dodge, and rolls the corresponding skill:

- Fighting back: the defender must achieve a higher level of success than the attacker to avoid the blow and deal damage instead. If the levels are equal, the attacker wins.
- Dodging: the defender avoids the attack if they achieve a level of success equal to or higher than the attacker. Ties favour the dodger.
- If both sides fail, nothing happens.

Combat rolls cannot be pushed.

### Extreme Success in Combat

When an attacker achieves an Extreme success (and the defender did not do better), the attack deals maximum weapon damage plus maximum Damage Bonus. If the weapon impales, such as a knife, a spear or a bullet, roll the weapon's damage once more and add it.

### Outnumbered

Once a character has fought back or dodged during a round, every further melee attack against them in that round is made with one bonus die.

### Fighting Manoeuvres

Instead of dealing damage, a character can attempt a manoeuvre: disarm, knock down, grapple, push or hold. Compare Build: for each point by which the target's Build exceeds the attacker's, the attacker takes one penalty die (up to two). If the target's Build is three or more points higher, the manoeuvre is impossible.

### Firearms

- Shooting is resolved with the appropriate Firearms skill; the target cannot dodge or fight back against a bullet, but a target diving for cover imposes a penalty die on the shooter.
- Point-blank range (within a number of feet equal to one fifth of the shooter's DEX) grants a bonus die.
- Firing two or three shots from a handgun in one round imposes a penalty die on each shot.
- Beyond a weapon's base range the difficulty rises: up to twice the base range requires a Hard success, up to four times requires an Extreme success.

### Damage

Weapons and falls deal damage in dice. As a rough scale: minor harm 1D3, moderate 1D6, severe 1D10, deadly 2D10, terminal 4D10, and a fall from a great height 8D10. Unarmed humans deal 1D3 plus Damage Bonus.

## Hit Points, Wounds and Healing

### Losing Hit Points

Damage reduces Hit Points. Hit Points never drop below zero.

- A Major Wound is a single hit that deals damage equal to or greater than half of the character's maximum Hit Points. The character must immediately succeed at a CON roll or fall unconscious.
- A character at 0 Hit Points without a Major Wound is unconscious.
- A character at 0 Hit Points who has a Major Wound is dying: they must succeed at a CON roll at the end of each following round or die. Successful First Aid stabilises a dying character.
- A single hit that exceeds the character's maximum Hit Points kills outright (Keeper Rulebook, via secondary sources).

### First Aid and Medicine

- A successful First Aid roll restores 1 Hit Point and wakes an unconscious character. It must be applied soon after the injury.
- A successful Medicine roll restores 1D3 Hit Points; it takes at least an hour and requires medical supplies.

### Natural Healing

Without a Major Wound, a character recovers 1 Hit Point per day of rest. With a Major Wound, the character makes a weekly CON roll: a success heals 1D3 Hit Points (2D3 on an Extreme success). The Major Wound is removed once Hit Points reach at least half of maximum or after an Extreme success on a healing roll.

## Investigator Development

At the end of a scenario, each skill that was used successfully during play and marked with an experience check can improve. For each checked skill, roll 1D100: if the result is higher than the current skill value, add 1D10 to the skill. Skills cannot rise through experience beyond what the Keeper allows, and Cthulhu Mythos never improves this way.

## Running the Game: Keeper Principles

- Call for rolls only when failure matters; otherwise let competent investigators succeed.
- State the stakes before the roll and honour the dice afterwards. The narration must follow the rolled outcome, not replace it.
- Let failure move the story forward: a failed search can still reveal a clue at a cost, or bring danger closer.
- Give clues freely when investigators look in the right place; hide the meaning, not the evidence.
- Build horror through the unseen: sounds, smells, traces and implications come before any monster appears.
- Never decide what an investigator thinks or does, except for involuntary actions after a failed Sanity roll or during insanity.
